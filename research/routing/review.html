Routing: drawing, part photo or text

Routing research for the case study, 2026-11-01. Measured on a MacBook Pro M1 Pro with no network. The full write-up is README.md.

The labelled set has 244 items. The router sees 234 distinct requests: 20 questions with no attachment, 204 drawing and photo files and 10 files that fit no case. The other 10 items send a message with a file from the set, and because the router ignores the message, they route exactly like their files and are counted once. The percentages on the layer cards are shares of the 204 drawings and photos, with the probe's folds grouped by source; the third layer's share is 2.5% by report page, 2.9% by source, 3.9% one source out. A card time is the median cost of that layer's own step. Every time on this page is reconstructed, not timed end to end: a request gets a 1 ms allowance for an image's file-type check (not measured), the measured layer-1 check of each PDF, 32 ms to load or render the picture and embed it with DINOv2, under 0.1 ms for the regression, and the file's own Qwen3-VL 8B call.

Of the drawings and photos, the third layer saw an old engraving of an air compressor section, an NTSB photo of a cable on a blue background, an NTSB X-ray image of a cable fitting, a second X-ray image of that fitting, an NTSB X-ray image of a threaded fitting and a photo inside a broken VCR, and it got all 6 right. It also saw two logos from the reports and an NTSB report page sent as an image, and called them something else. The logos took 1.2 s because they are small images, and the model's time grows with the image area it reads. The scanned report page, with and without its OCR layer, reached the third layer too, but the vision-language models were not run on the four files added to the set after the model runs, so that answer is not measured. Two files that fit no case still got a case: an NTSB report page the probe called a drawing at 0.900, just at the threshold, and the physics diagram at 0.99. Qwen3-VL 8B also calls the diagram a drawing when asked alone, because its question lists a dimensioned diagram under drawing.

Why not use one model alone

On the drawings and photos, the probe alone made no mistake, and it is cheaper than the full router. The other two layers are there for the files that fit no case, because a probe trained on two classes can only answer drawing or photo.

With the probe alone, all 10 files that fit no case went to the drawing case, at drawing probabilities from 0.58 to 0.99, so the drawing reader would try to read drawing values out of reports and logos. Layer 1 catches the three report PDFs. Layer 3 catches the logos and one report page, while the other report page scored 0.900, just above the threshold. Layer 3 also gets the drawings and photos the probe is least sure of, the 6 listed above. The probe's own answer for those 6 was right too, so on this set layer 3 changed no answer for a drawing or photo, and it is there as a check on unfamiliar files. Once there are labelled examples of documents, logos and charts, the probe can learn a third class for them, and layer 3 would then see fewer files.

LiquidAI's decision model d1-2B can answer "something else" itself, so it was also tried alone and behind the structure rules, with no probe. Behind the rules it kept 7 of the 10 files that fit no case out, one more than the recommended router, because it called both report pages sent as images "something else", and it needs no labelled training. But it called one photo, corroded lock bolts, a drawing at 0.48, and it costs about ten times as much: 1,146 ms per drawing or photo on average and 650 ms at the median, against 111 and 34 ms, with a 95th percentile of 4.3 s. At a million images that is about 13 days of one laptop, against about 1.2 to 1.6 days for the recommended router. It also needs 2.4 GB of memory, against 0.1 GB for the probe, and its licence allows commercial use only below 10 million US dollars of annual revenue. So the structure rules and d1-2B are a reasonable two-layer design for low volumes once the licence is checked, and for a million documents the probe in front is what keeps the cost down. Keep the structure rules in front in either design, because they decide a CAD PDF in 14 to 37 ms without any model.

The labelled set (244 items) is small and partly made by code, and several options reach 100% on it, so it shows which designs fail and why more than it ranks the ones that work.

How layer 1 reads a PDF

A PDF page stores what it shows as drawing instructions. Text is stored as characters in a font, a photo or a scan is stored as one embedded picture made of pixels, and lines and shapes are stored as vector paths. A vector path is a short list of geometry, such as straight lines, curves and rectangles given by their end points, that the viewer draws with a pen (a stroke), fills with a colour, or both.

A CAD program exports every outline, hidden line, dimension line, arrowhead and shaded face of a drawing as paths, so a drawing sheet has hundreds or thousands of them. For example, page 1 of NIST CTC-01 has 1,797 paths, of which 1,263 are filled shapes (the shaded 3D views), 261 are stroked lines and 273 are both, and together they hold 8,908 straight pieces and 583 curves. A report page uses paths only for a few lines and boxes, and a scanned drawing has none, because the scanner saved the whole sheet as one picture.

Layer 1 opens the PDF with PyMuPDF and reads page 1. page.get_cdrawings() reads the page's drawing instructions and returns one entry per path, so the rule only has to count the entries. The rule also adds up the page area under embedded pictures (page.get_image_info()) and counts the characters of text (page.get_text()). A first page with 300 or more paths and less than 20% of its area under pictures is a drawing.

Otherwise the rule reads the first ten pages and counts two kinds: a CAD sheet, with 300 or more paths and little picture, and a page of prose, with 1,000 or more characters and not one big picture. A PDF with at least as many CAD sheets as prose pages is a drawing, which routes a drawing package that starts with a cover and a notes sheet. A PDF with more prose pages is a document. A page that is one big picture never counts as prose, so the hidden text layer of an OCRed scan cannot make it a document, and the probe judges the picture instead.

Counting thousands of paths is most of the cost. The check took 14 to 37 ms on a CAD PDF (median 21 ms), about 1 ms on a one-picture PDF, 7 to 36 ms on a report and 50 ms on the drawing package, where it reads all ten pages.

An earlier version of the rule called a PDF a document when page 1 had 1,500 characters or any of the first ten pages had 1,000, which sent the drawing package and the OCRed report page to the document bin. Comparing counts is also why report 18-061 is still a document: one of its first ten pages is a 310-path chart, among eight pages of prose. On page 1 the cut point of 300 sits between 17 and 613 paths. Two kinds of PDF would still fool the rule, and neither is in the labelled set: a page whose text was converted to outlines, which has every letter as paths and no characters, and a report made mostly of large vector charts. Both would be called drawings. The reason the router records with each decision includes the path count (for example "vector PDF, 1797 paths"), so such files can be found in the logs and added to the labelled set.

The rules are scored on the half of the files their cut points were not fitted on, and an undecided file counts as wrong. The probes are scored with out-of-fold predictions, the folds grouped by source. "Out of domain" has two tests: the 19 Wikimedia Commons files when a probe is trained on the NIST and NTSB files only, and the 109 NIST files when it is trained on the Commons drawings and every photo. In every probe, the image model is frozen and only the logistic regression is trained.

Precision for a case is the share of the files an option sent to that case that belong there, and recall is the share of that case's files it sent there, so an undecided or "something else" answer lowers recall but not precision. The rules' rows cover all 204 files, including the half their cut points were fitted on. On the held-out half alone they score 51.9% with EXIF and 30.6% without, against 77.2% and 61.4% when the halves were grouped by report page, so the cut points move with the files they are fitted on. The vision-language and decision models were not run on the 2 drawing files added to the set after the model runs, so their rows cover 202 files. The router's times are reconstructed from measured steps.

The probes' expected calibration error (ECE) is 0.009 to 0.023. With no mistakes, though, it can only show that a probe is less sure than it is right, not how it behaves when it is wrong, and by the same measure the vision-language models score 0.000 to 0.005. The router puts its threshold on the probe because the probe runs on every image anyway and costs 32 ms, while a vision-language model costs seconds.

How the threshold changes the recommended router

No threshold from 0.5 to 0.995 made a mistake on the labelled requests, so this set cannot tell 0.8 from 0.9, and 0.9 is a default, not a measured best. A higher threshold sends more files to the third layer, which costs time, and keeps more files that fit no case out of the drawing and photo cases. Between 0.8 and 0.9, the files that start to escalate are an NTSB photo of a cable on a blue background, an NTSB X-ray image of a cable fitting and an old engraving of an air compressor section. The share escalated also depends on how the probe's folds are grouped, so each row gives the range over three groupings. The threshold should be set again on the customer's files.

How the labelled set was made

No item in the set was invented. 107 items are real files, 107 were made by code from those real files, and 30 are messages written by hand; the drawing package also carries a cover and a notes sheet written for this set. The script experiments/routing/build_dataset.py makes every item with fixed random seeds, so two builds give the same bytes. The code-made copies test what real uploads will do to a drawing: a low-quality JPEG, a slight rotation, a crop, a phone photo of a printout, and a scan.

The labelled set page shows all 244 items with how each one was made. The files are in evals/gold/routes/items/, and evals/gold/routes/routes.jsonl has one line per item with its label, source and note.

One cleaning step used a model under test. Zero-shot SigLIP 2 flagged four "photos" that were black frames stored as pictures in a report, and the mask filter was tightened to drop them, so the 100% rows are on a set one of the evaluated models helped clean.

Where approaches went wrong

Open questions, with the default chosen

  • An attachment that fits no case (a whole report, a page of text) goes to the text case with the file as context, and the answer says so. The port names this outcome unsupported, so the choice changes in one place.
  • A drawing sent with a question ("what is the tolerance on hole A?") goes to the drawing case, which returns the JSON and can answer from it. The user's text never changes the case.
  • A PDF is routed by its first page, or by its first ten pages when layer 1 counts them. A PDF that mixes drawings and photos would need per-page routing.
  • Which image model the probe uses is not settled by this set. DINOv2-small is the default and the probe adapter stays swappable; the probe must be trained on the customer's own drawing family before any of the 100% rows apply.

Not tested

  • The vision-language and decision models on the four files added to the set after the model runs: each needs a 2 to 7 GB model load. run_models.py --update with each model's name fills them in.
  • Cloudflare's Clef-flash, an open-weight (Apache-2.0) decision model that reads images: its 4-bit build is 6.2 GB and was not downloaded for this study. It is the closest local match to Jev and worth one run.
  • The 8-bit build of LiquidAI d1-2B: stopped at 175 of 210 files when the laptop ran low on memory.

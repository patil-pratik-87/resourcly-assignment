Drawing-to-JSON research for the case study, 2026-10-31.

How should we read every value off the NIST drawings?

Recommendation: try Proposal 1 first, and measure it against Proposal 2 as the baseline.

Use the PDF's own lines to find each annotation, then have Qwen3-VL 8B read one small picture at a time. In tests it was the only setup that named every GD&T symbol correctly (6 of 6).

The write-up is README.md beside this page. The notebook that finds the annotation boxes is experiments/drawing/callout_boxes.ipynb.

What these drawings are, in plain words

Each sheet shows a 3D-looking view of a machined part. The blue writing around it tells a machinist what to make:

  • Dimensions: sizes such as 4X Ø.281 ±.008, which means "4 holes, diameter .281 inch, plus or minus .008".
  • Feature control frames: the rows of little boxes. Box 1 is a symbol for the kind of rule (position, flatness, profile...). Box 2 is the allowed error. The other boxes are datums, the reference surfaces A, B and C that you measure from.
  • Leaders: thin lines that tie each note to the feature it is about.

Three facts decide how to read them:

  1. The writing is drawn as shapes, not stored as text. This holds on 29 of the 30 pages, so nothing can simply copy it out of the PDF. FTC-07 is the exception.
  2. The writing is tilted and slanted, because it lies on the faces of the 3D view.
  3. All of it, and only it, is pure blue. That gives a free, exact way to find where every annotation is.

How the crops were made

No AI is used to find the crops. The steps are:

  1. Read the PDF's drawing instructions. PyMuPDF lists every line and filled shape on the page with its colour and position.
  2. Keep only pure blue. On these sheets only annotations are pure blue, so this throws away the part, its shading and the axis arrows. The letters and digits show up as small filled shapes, and the frame boxes as thin blue lines.
  3. Group shapes that sit close together. Two shapes that are within about two letter-heights of each other join the same group, so one group is one annotation.
  4. Add the frame lines. Any blue line whose two ends both sit next to a group is added to it. These are the walls of the tolerance-frame boxes. Groups that overlap are merged.
  5. Cut the picture. The page is rendered at 300 dpi and each group's box is cut out with a margin of about 8 mm sideways and 6 mm up and down.

There were two manual parts in the test:

  • The margin. My first, tight boxes cut off the symbol box at the left of some frames, and the model then got that symbol wrong or skipped the frame. Widening the margin fixed it.
  • Which crops to test. From the 58 boxes found on CTC-01, CTC-03 and FTC-06, I picked 8 to send to the model: 6 tolerance frames covering position, profile and flatness, and 2 plain dimensions.

So "right on all tested crops" means those 8, not every box. Proposal 1 would run every box, and the locator's own errors (a split frame, or two notes merged) would count against it in the score.

MechVL-4B, SFT and RL, against Qwen3-VL

MechVL-4B is Qwen3-VL-4B fine-tuned on mechanical drawings. SFT answers directly. RL writes out its reasoning first and then answers. I ran both, plus Qwen3-VL 4B and 8B, through the same test: the same 8 crops and 3 whole pages, each once without and once with the OCR text added to the prompt as a hint.

Crops: checks passed out of 46 (each value, symbol, datum letters, circled-M count, special symbols). Pages: F1 on the numbers, where 1.00 is perfect.

  • Neither MechVL version beats Qwen3-VL 8B, and on crops both score below plain Qwen3-VL 4B, the model they were trained from.
  • Neither fixes the gaps we hoped it would: no model reads the counterbore or depth symbols, or the circled M on datums B and C.
  • Both see nothing in the profile frame ⌓ 1.25 A B C, which both Qwen models read.
  • RL invents frames on two plain dimensions, draws the position symbol as the concentricity symbol ◎, and is about 8 times slower than the 8B. It ran out of tokens while still reasoning on 2 of 16 crops.
  • The OCR hint does not help on crops: every model lost 1 to 5 checks. On whole pages it stops the 4B models from repeating themselves, but they then mostly copy the OCR list, mistakes included.
  • No model names the GD&T symbols on a whole page.

So the recommendation stays the same: Proposal 1 with Qwen3-VL 8B, without the OCR hint. MechVL is dropped from Proposal 5.

The proposals, ranked

Every proposal runs fully offline on the laptop and is scored the same way: hand-written correct values for a few sheets, then recall (share of real values found), precision (share of outputs that are right) and F1 (their balance), plus a separate score for symbols.

1 - recommended first

Find each annotation from the PDF lines, then read one crop at a time

What it does:

  1. Use the blue shapes in the PDF to box every annotation.
  2. Cut a small picture around each one and ask Qwen3-VL 8B to copy it out as JSON. The prompt explains the GD&T symbols.
  3. Follow each leader line to see which feature the note points at.
  4. Read the notes and title block directly, because they are real text.

Why it should work: each picture is small, so the model sees the symbols clearly and cannot get lost or start repeating itself. It is the only setup that got the symbols right in testing.

Where it can fail: boxing errors on crowded sheets, rare symbols (counterbore, depth, circled M on each datum), and "belongs to" only at the level of "this hole", not "hole number 3". Reading all 11 drawings takes 20 to 45 minutes.

About 1.5-2 h to build. Licences: Qwen3-VL Apache-2.0; PyMuPDF AGPL-3.0.

2 - baseline to beat

Show the whole page to Qwen3-VL 8B

What it does: one picture of the sheet, one request: "list every annotation".

Why it might work: it is the simplest possible pipeline, and it found most numbers on the simpler sheets.

Where it fails (seen in testing): it never names the GD&T symbols; on the crowded FTC-06 it misread .625 as .825 and then repeated one line until it ran out of room; it cannot tell what each note points at.

About 30 min to build.

3

OCR reads the text, the VLM only reads the symbols

What it does: a fast OCR engine reads all text with positions in about 1 second. The VLM is asked one short question per frame: "which symbol is this?". Rules then regroup stacked tolerances and fix Ø read as 0.

Why: it is very fast, and the GPU is only used for symbols, which suits the "million drawings" scale story.

Risk: more moving parts and glue rules, and it is tight inside 4 hours.

About 2.5-3 h. Apache-2.0 throughout.

4

Blue-only page cut into tiles (untested)

What it does: erase the part, keep only the blue writing, cut the page into 4 to 6 overlapping tiles, and read each tile.

Why: no boxing code is needed, and the tiles are clean.

Risk: notes get cut at tile edges, duplicates appear in the overlaps, dense tiles can still loop, and "belongs to" is lost once the part is erased.

About 1 h. This is the fallback if the boxing in Proposal 1 proves fragile.

5

Same as 1, but with a drawing-trained model

What it does: swap Qwen3-VL 8B for eDOCr2 (MIT), an OCR tool with a GD&T frame reader.

Why: it may fix the rare symbols the general model misses.

Risk: it is not proven on drawings like these, and its Keras stack suits Colab better than this laptop.

Tested: MechVL-4B, the other candidate, did worse than Qwen3-VL 8B (see the box above), so it is dropped.

About 30 min once Proposal 1 exists.

Ruled out:

  • Docling and similar converters: tested, they lose everything.
  • Tesseract and EasyOCR: they cannot handle 30° text.
  • Werk24 and Mistral OCR: cloud only.
  • The published fine-tuned systems: no weights released, and no labels to train our own.
  • Surya and Chandra: their licences limit use by companies above a revenue cap.

Decisions taken

  • First experiment: Proposal 1, crops located from the PDF lines and read by Qwen3-VL 8B.
  • Baseline: Proposal 2, the whole page to Qwen3-VL 8B.
  • Runtime: mlx-vlm, which ran every test here.
  • MechVL-4B: tested, and dropped.
  • "Every value": includes the title block, the sheet details and the general notes. It leaves out the XYZ axis-marker letters and the view labels.
  • Hand-labelled answers: CTC-01, CTC-03 and FTC-06 in full, plus a check of ten values on each of the other eight drawings.

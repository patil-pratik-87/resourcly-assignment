Fault detection and chat research for the case study, 2026-11-01.

How should we say what is wrong with a failed part?

Recommendation: find the closest report figures with DINOv2-base alone, then have Qwen3-VL 8B write a cited answer from the top 3 figures and their report text.

Retrieval works: with the photo's own figure hidden, DINOv2 puts the right report first for 95.7% of 138 photos and the right failure mode in the top 3 for 94.2%, the best of the signals compared on both, in under 0.1 s. One image model, no fusion. The router's smaller DINOv2-small was measured too and is worse on every held-out score, so it is not shared yet. The answer step does not work yet: 3 cases, all wrong, about 2 minutes each.

The write-up is README.md beside this page.

The task, in plain words

A user sends a photo of a broken part. The agent must say what is wrong and what to do, using only the three NTSB materials laboratory reports, and then answer follow-up questions.

ReportSubjectPagesFigures
16-006Mooney M20TN throttle and mixture controls1412
18-061Piper PA-28R-201 wing main spars4029
24-013Cessna 177RG fuel servo throttle arm65

The pipeline has five steps, each measured on its own:

  1. Ingest: cut each PDF into figures (image, caption, the paragraphs that cite it) and paragraphs.
  2. Describe: Qwen3-VL 8B lists what it sees and the image type.
  3. Retrieve: rank every report figure by DINOv2 similarity to the photo.
  4. Gate: answer "not in the reports" when the photo is far from every figure. Drawings never get here: the router sends them to the drawing case.
  5. Answer: Qwen3-VL 8B sees the photo, the top 2 figures and the text of the top 3, and returns findings with sources (such as 18-061 fig05) and actions marked "stated in the report" or "inferred".

A text question skips the photo steps: BM25 finds paragraphs and the same model answers, citing them.

How it was measured

There are no real photos of these parts, so the test photos are made from the report figures. Each of the 46 figures is copied 3 times with a random crop, rotation, colour change, blur, downscale and heavy JPEG: 138 positives. 23 negatives: page 1 of the 11 NIST drawings and 12 unrelated photos. Each figure has a hand label from the report text: its section (for example the N106ER spar) and a coarse failure mode (fatigue 54, no defect 48, overstress 15, jammed control 15, heat damage 6).

The failure gold in evals/gold/failure/ holds the same 149 images. Its 5 unrelated images (report logos, text pages, a diagram) replace the 12 unrelated photos used here, so the gate floor must be checked there again.

Two settings:

  • Copy: the photo's own figure stays in the index. This only checks that the changes do not break matching.
  • Held out: the photo's own figure is hidden, so the best possible hit is another figure of the same section. A real user's photo is never a report figure, so this is the setting that decides.

Each score asks one question about the list of report figures that search returns for a photo, ranked from closest to least close.

  • report@1: is the first figure in the list from the right report? There are only three reports, so this checks whether the agent at least opens the right report.
  • section@k: is a figure of the same part from the same aircraft somewhere in the first k figures? A section is a group of figures about one part, such as "the N106ER wing spar" or "the mixture control". section@1 looks only at the first figure; section@5 looks at the first five.
  • mode@k: is a figure showing the same kind of failure somewhere in the first k figures? The failure modes are fatigue, overstress, jammed control, heat damage, or "no defect". The part doesn't have to match, only the kind of damage.

Example. The photo is a damaged copy of a fatigue crack on the N106ER spar. Search returns:

  1. Another N106ER spar figure, showing fatigue
  2. An N104ER spar figure, showing fatigue
  3. A microstructure figure from the same report, showing no defect
  • report@1: yes, the first figure is from the spar report.
  • section@1: yes, the first figure is the same part.
  • mode@1: yes, the first figure shows fatigue.
  • mode@3: yes, at least one of the first three shows fatigue.

If the first figure had been the microstructure one, report@1 would still be yes, but section@1 and mode@1 would be no. mode@3 would still be yes, because a fatigue figure is in the top three.

Why mode@3 matters most. The answer step reads the top 3 figures, so a correct answer is possible as long as one of them shows the right kind of failure. That is why "DINOv2 gets mode@3 94.2%" is the headline. It means that for 94 of every 100 photos, the evidence the model needs is in front of it.

Search needs no language model, so every option ran on all 161 cases, in 5 to 15 s each. The describe and answer steps ran on 3 cases only.

  • Image to image works; image to text does not. Matching the photo to captions finds the exact figure only 18.8% of the time even when it is in the index, and adding captions to the fusion drops held-out section@1 from 60.9% to 47.8%.
  • DINOv2 alone is enough. It is best on report@1 and mode@3. SigLIP 2 and the fusion are within 6 photos out of 138 (the fusion leads on mode@1 by 5) and would add a second image model, so both are dropped.
  • The exact section is hard (64.5% at rank 1 for DINOv2) because many figures of one report show the same spar or control. The answer step sees the top 3, where the right failure mode is 94.2% of the time.
  • BM25 on the describe step's words was never measured at scale, because it needs the model, so it is off for photos. Text questions still use BM25.

Can retrieval share the router's DINOv2-small?

The router's probe uses facebook/dinov2-small and its pooled class-token vector (384 numbers). Retrieval was measured with DINOv2-base and the class token plus the mean of the patch tokens. Both were run again here with DINOv2-small, same 161 cases, same 224 px centre crop.

DINOv2-small, pooled class token (the router's vector)97.8%90.6%58.0%81.2%75.4%90.6%0.308 / 0.285

  • Small is worse on every held-out score. With the router's vector it gets 7 fewer reports right at rank 1, 9 fewer sections and 5 fewer failure modes in the top 3, out of 138. Each gap is small, but all point the same way, and its report@1 is the lowest of any image option measured.
  • Small also loses the clean gate. Its best unrelated photo (0.308) scores above its worst positive (0.285), so any floor that rejects every unrelated photo also turns away a real one (99.3% kept).
  • Speed is not the issue. Small embedded all 207 images in 3.8 s; base was also well under 0.1 s per photo.
  • Decision: keep DINOv2-base for retrieval. To reach one model file, measure the router's probe on DINOv2-base (seconds, no language model) rather than shrink retrieval.

The gate: can similarity say "not in the reports"?

SigLIP 2 (dropped): best similarity per photo (bar = lowest to highest, tick = median)

DINOv2 (chosen): best similarity per photo, dashed = the 0.28 floor

  • This is the cost of one model. SigLIP 2 separated every negative, only just: best drawing 0.718, worst held-out positive 0.722. DINOv2 overlaps: a floor above every negative (0.421, set by a drawing) keeps only 94.9% of positives.
  • Chosen: a DINOv2 floor of 0.28. Drawings never reach this step, because the router sends them to the drawing case. Unrelated photos reach at most 0.271 and the worst positive is 0.294, so 0.28 keeps all 138 positives and rejects all 12 unrelated photos.
  • The margin is small and rests on 12 photos, so the floor must be checked on real photos of other parts. The describe step's "unrelated" label stays as a second check; it was never scored on negatives.

  • Right evidence, wrong verdict. Retrieval put the right figure first every time, and Qwen3-VL 8B still said the photo matched nothing. All 3 were no-defect reference figures, which the answer prompt does not handle well.

Limits

  • The answer step is unproven: 3 cases, all wrong, about 2 minutes each. It needs a scored set of photos and questions, checked for the right report, failure mode and valid citations.
  • The chat was not scored at all.
  • Every positive is a report figure, changed by code. A real photo of a cracked spar will look less like the report than these do, so the held-out numbers are an upper bound.
  • "No defect" is ambiguous: the right answer to a photo of an intact reference spar is not settled.
  • The gate floor rests on 12 unrelated photos, with a margin of 0.023.
  • Two DINOv2 sizes for now. The router keeps DINOv2-small, which was measured there; retrieval needs base. The router's probe has not been tried on base.
  • 46 figures are searched by brute force; a million documents need an approximate nearest neighbour index behind the same interface.
